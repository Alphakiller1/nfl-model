Ratings · 04

Power ratings

Two independent estimates of team strength, side by side. Rating is the opponent-adjusted scoring-margin solve: points relative to an average team on a neutral field, so the projected neutral margin is the difference of two ratings and the host adds 1.20. Off and Def come from the separate matchup model, and Eff is exactly their sum. The two columns are not meant to be equal — the published margin is the 50/50 blend of them, and a team where they disagree sharply is a team whose record and whose per-play efficiency tell different stories, which is worth seeing rather than averaging away. Proj W is the mean of 20,000 simulated seasons over the real 2026 fixture list, not a league-average schedule.

Units · 05

Offensive & defensive power rankings

Pts is points per game above an average unit, on the same scale as the overall rating — an offence at +3.1 and a defence at +1.4 add to a +4.5 team, and they add exactly, because both come from the one matchup fit rather than from three models that disagree at the edges. Component rates are opponent-adjusted and coloured by league percentile. A defensive row records what opponents did, so a low EPA allowed and a high sack rate are both good defence; the colouring already accounts for that.

Projected division winners

Every regular-season game on the real 2026 fixture list, replayed 20,000 times from the same margins the board publishes. Division ties are broken by head-to-head, then division record, then conference record, then a coin flip — the later NFL tiebreakers are not implemented, and ordering by power rating instead would quietly hand every tie to the favourite. Ratings do not change during a simulated season, nobody gets injured, and draws are independent, so these tails are thinner than reality’s.

Rec is the mean simulated record, Div the chance of winning the division, PO the chance of reaching the playoffs. The bar is Div drawn to scale. Division chances sum to 100% within each division and playoff chances to seven per conference, by construction.

Method · 08

How the numbers are built

Every constant below was measured on 2,383 out-of-sample games rather than assumed, and the sweeps that came back flat are labelled as flat. Reproduce with python scripts/fit_matrix.py.

The matchup model

One fit produces everything: margin, total, scoreline and both unit rankings. It predicts the points one team scores, from that team’s offence and its opponent’s defence.

points(A vs B) = intercept
   + Σ gₓ · ( offₓ(A) + defₓ(B) )
   + home_field

Offence and defence share a coefficient because they measure the same per-play quantity from opposite sides. Given them free coefficients instead and def_epa fits positive — allowing more EPA per play “improves” the margin — because EPA and first-down rate correlate at r = 0.83 and the pair splits the effect with opposite signs. The symmetric form costs 0.016 points of MAE and every sign it produces is the one football says it should be.

Power ratings

Opponent-adjusted scoring margin, solved iteratively, home field removed before rating so a soft home schedule earns nothing.

Margins pass through cap · tanh(margin / cap), but the cap barely matters here: 7.0% of NFL games are decided by 28+ against 36% in FBS, and the sweep moves MAE by 0.004 points from cap 17 to no cap at all. It is kept to bound a freak result, not because it contributes.

Totals & scorelines

The raw model total is over-dispersed — regressing actual on predicted gives a slope near 0.63 — so it is shrunk 30% toward the league mean, which lifts the slope to 0.91.

Scores are algebra: home = (total + margin) / 2. They inherit the error of both models, so read a scoreline as a centre of mass. A total marked * is the league mean, not a modelled figure.

Point-in-time

A game in week W is forecast only from completed games before W, blended with earlier seasons on a shrinkage of 6 games. Coefficients are fitted leave-one-season-out, so no game is ever scored by a model that saw it — the only fit whose MAE is comparable with the market’s, because the market never saw the answer either.

Where this is weakest

The model is 0.44 points behind the closing line overall, and the gap is widest late (weeks 10–18: +0.55) rather than early (weeks 1–4: +0.39). That is the opposite of the college case, and the reason is availability: by November the market is pricing injuries and roster news this repo does not model at all, while in September there is less of it to know.

Three families the inherited research prior asserted — early-down pass efficiency, red-zone conversion and special-teams field position — are not derivable from the weekly team box score and are not in the model. They were dropped rather than shipped as decorative weights.
